How to choose a website company for your HVAC, plumbing or roofing business (2026)

By Jared KolsunCo-Founder & CTONovember 18, 2026

Full disclosure: we run a website company, Bright Automations, and we are one of the options in this guide. I have tried to be fair to the others. Every option below is the right call for some owners, and ours is not the right call for everyone.

If you run an HVAC, plumbing or roofing business, you have probably been pitched a website more times than you can count. Most of those pitches sound the same. So instead of telling you who is best, here is how the main options actually differ, what each one is good at, and the questions I would ask any of them before signing anything.

The four main options

Almost every choice falls into one of four groups: build it yourself, hire a freelancer, hire a web design agency, or pick a company that builds and runs the site for a monthly price. We are in that last group.

Option 1: build it yourself

Website builders like Wix, Squarespace and GoDaddy let you pick a template, drop in your photos and publish.

What is good about it. It costs the least in dollars. You are in full control, you can start tonight, and you never wait on anyone to change a phone number or a photo.

What to watch for. You are the designer, the writer and the person setting up everything Google needs. That takes hours most owners would rather spend on jobs. It is also easy to end up with one page that says a little about everything. People search for a specific job in a specific town, so a site with a page for each service and each town you serve has a better shot at those searches.

Option 2: hire a freelancer

A freelance designer builds the site for you, usually for a one-time fee.

What is good about it. You get a real person who builds something custom, and a good freelancer can be a great value. If you already know someone whose work you like, this can be the simplest path.

What to watch for. Everything depends on one person. Ask what happens when they are busy, on vacation or move on. Ask who hosts the site, who holds the domain login, and what a change costs after launch.

Option 3: hire a web design agency

An agency puts a team on your project: design, writing and often marketing too.

What is good about it. A good agency brings strategy, a custom design and people who can also run ads or other marketing. For a larger company with several locations or a big ad budget, that depth can be worth it.

What to watch for. The cost is usually upfront, before you have seen the finished site. And too often, an agency builds the site, sends the invoice and then gets hard to reach. Get in writing what is included after launch, how changes are billed and how long the contract runs.

Questions to ask any website company

Whoever you talk to, including us, these questions separate a good fit from a bad one.

  1. Can I see the site before I pay? If not, ask to see live sites they built for businesses like yours.
  2. Who owns the domain and the content? It should be you. Get it in writing.
  3. What happens if I cancel? Ask what you keep and what goes away.
  4. What does a change cost after launch? Your services, prices and photos will change. Find out if that is included or billed.
  5. Will each service and each town I serve get its own page? Those are the searches your customers make.
  6. Are the basics covered? Tap to call on phones, a contact form that actually sends, and the markup that tells Google who you are and where you work.
  7. How will I know it is working? Ask for a regular report on calls and traffic, not just a launch day email.
  8. Is there a setup fee or a long contract? Know what you are committing to before you start.

One red flag to walk away from

Anyone who guarantees you a top spot on Google. Nobody controls Google's rankings, and nobody can honestly promise you calls or a position. A good company tells you what it will build and how it will report on it, then lets the results speak.

So which should you pick?

If you enjoy building things yourself and have the evenings for it, a website builder is a fine start. If you know a freelancer you trust, that can work well. If you run a larger company and want a custom project with a marketing team behind it, look at agencies. If you would rather see a finished site first and have someone else handle it for a flat monthly price, that is the option we built.
