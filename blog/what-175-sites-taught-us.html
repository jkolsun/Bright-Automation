What 175 client sites taught us about getting found on Google

By Jared KolsunCo-Founder & CTONovember 4, 2026

We wanted to know what the sites getting found on Google had in common. So we crawled 175 of our client sites and compared them.

I expected the answer to be design, or speed, or how many reviews the business had. The clearest pattern was simpler than any of that. It was size. Sites with 10 or more pages got about twice the Google visitors of the smaller ones.

That is a pattern, not a promise. A bigger site does not guarantee a single call. But once you think about how people actually search, it makes a lot of sense.

Nobody searches for "contractor"

People search for the job they need done and the place they need it. "Deck builder" plus their town. "Emergency drain cleaning near me." "Roof repair" plus the county they live in.

A one page site has to answer all of those searches with the same page. Usually it ends up saying a little about everything and not enough about anything. Google has a hard time deciding which search it is the best answer for.

A site with its own page for each service, and its own page for each town you work in, gives Google a clear answer for each one. I think of it like doors. A one page site is one door. A site with a page for every service and every town has a lot more of them.

Which pages actually count

Not every page helps. Ten pages that all say the same thing with a different town name swapped in are not ten doors. The pages that count are the ones a customer would actually want to land on.

A page for each service. If you do roof repair, roof replacement and gutters, those are three different searches and three different customers. Each one deserves a page that explains that job, shows your work and makes it easy to reach you.

A page for each town you really serve. Only the places you actually work. A town page should tell someone in that town that you work there and what you do there.

The basics on every page. Tap to call that works on a phone, a contact form that sends, and the information Google needs to understand who you are and where you work.

What we do about it

This is why every site we build gets its own pages for your services and for the towns you serve. It is built into how we work, not an upgrade.

Before any build goes out, it runs through an automated quality check. It confirms every page loads, tap to call works, the contact form sends, tracking is in place, the site carries LocalBusiness schema so Google can read who you are, and there are at least four town pages. A site that fails any of those does not go out. I wrote more about that process in how we build a website before the owner pays.

Your business also changes. You add a service, or you start taking jobs two towns over. On the Website plan changes are unlimited, so you text us and we add the page. Every client also gets a monthly report on calls, traffic and next steps, with one to three recommended changes you can approve with one tap.

How long it takes to show up

Pages do not start bringing in visitors the day they go live. In our experience a new site typically takes 6 to 8 weeks to show up on Google, or 4 to 6 weeks with SEO Premier, which fully manages your Google Business Profile alongside the site. That is what we typically see, not a guarantee, and nobody can honestly promise you a spot on Google.

A quick check for your own site

Count your pages. Then write down every service you want calls for and every town you want to be found in. If that list is longer than your page count, there are searches your customers are making right now that your site has no page for.
