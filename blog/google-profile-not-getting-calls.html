Your Google profile isn't getting calls? Check these 6 things first

By Jared KolsunCo-Founder & CTONovember 20, 2026

One question keeps showing up in the small business forums this month: "My Google Business Profile is set up, so why isn't it getting calls?" I see the same thing when we look at new clients' profiles. Most owners treat their Google profile like a business card they set up once. The wrong services are listed, the service area doesn't match where they actually work, there are a handful of old reviews and no real photos.

Google tends to show the profile that looks complete and active, not necessarily the best business. The good news is that almost everything below is free and takes an evening. Here are the six things I'd check, in this order.

1. Your primary category

Google uses your categories to decide which searches you show up for, and the primary one matters most. Pick the most specific category for the jobs that make you money. "Roofing contractor" beats "contractor." Add secondary categories only for work you actually do.

2. Your services list

List the jobs you want to be hired for, in the words your customers use. If you want more drain cleaning calls and fewer one-off faucet repairs, make sure drain cleaning is on there and described clearly. A short, plain description for each service helps more than a long list of everything you could possibly do.

3. Your service area

Set your service area to the towns you actually drive to. If you list a whole state, you're telling Google you're everywhere and nowhere. If you leave off the town where half your best jobs come from, you're invisible there. Match it to reality, and update it when your routes change.

4. Recent reviews, from every customer

Ask every customer for a review, not just the ones you know were happy. Google's rules don't allow picking only the happy customers to ask, and a steady trickle of fresh reviews looks a lot more alive than fifty reviews from three years ago. Reply to every review, good or bad, in a calm and specific way. People read the replies.

5. Real photos, added regularly

Stock photos don't help anyone choose you. Photos from real jobs do: the before and after, the truck, the crew, the finished work. Add a few every month instead of twenty once. It's one of the easiest ways to show Google and customers that the business is active.

6. The same name, phone and website everywhere

Your business name, phone number and website should match exactly on your Google profile, your own website and every directory you're listed on. Small differences, like an old number on one listing or a slightly different name on another, make it harder for Google to be sure all of those listings are the same business.

Then look at your website

Your profile sends people to your website, so check it on your phone. Your number should be tappable right at the top, the page should load quickly, and there should be a page for each service you offer and each town you serve. When we crawled 175 of our client sites, the ones with 10 or more pages got about twice the Google visitors. I wrote up what those 175 sites taught us if you want the details.
